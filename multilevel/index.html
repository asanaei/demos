Multilevel modeling

Life satisfaction for simulated respondents, built from the World Values Survey. In four countries with 250 people each, one regression for everyone gets the sign of age wrong, and the slope of freedom of choice differs from country to country. In 65 countries with eight people each, the multilevel models beat fixed effects on AIC and BIC.

The R script that simulated the data

The respondents are simulated. The script under the R code draws age, gender, freedom of choice, and education from summaries of each country's sample in the seventh wave of the World Values Survey (Haerpfer et al. 2024). Life satisfaction comes from a latent score whose thresholds reproduce each country's real shares of answers 1 to 10, with slopes calibrated to the real ones, except that age has the same slope in every country, as the real data allow. The 65-country example applies the same method to every country and territory in the wave except Iraq, whose file copies the answers on financial satisfaction into the income question, and draws eight respondents from each. No real respondent appears in the data.
